## Understanding Heat-Related Illnesses

Extended heat waves and high humidity place severe stress on the body's internal cooling system. When dehydration sets in or outdoor exertion continues under oppressive conditions, heat exhaustion can rapidly degenerate into life-threatening **heat stroke**.

---

## Heat Exhaustion vs. Heat Stroke

It is critical to distinguish mild heat exhaustion from a medical emergency:

| Sign | Heat Exhaustion | Heat Stroke (Medical Emergency) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Skin Condition** | Heavy sweating, pale or clammy | Hot, red, dry skin (sweating has stopped) |
| **Body Temperature** | Normal or slightly elevated | Dangerously high (often above 40°C / 104°F) |
| **Mental State** | Tired, weak, dizziness, headache | Confusion, slurred speech, delirium, coma |
| **Pulse** | Fast and weak | Rapid and pounding |
| **Nausea** | Mild nausea or muscle cramps | Severe vomiting or seizures |

---

## First Aid for Heat Stroke

Heat stroke is a medical emergency. While medical help is summoned, you must cool the person down immediately.

1. **Move to Shade:** Immediately bring the casualty indoors or into deep shade away from direct sunlight.
2. **Loosen Tight Clothing:** Remove heavy jackets, socks, and constrictive outer layers to expose the skin to air circulation.
3. **Active Rapid Cooling:**
   - Sponge the entire body with cool (not freezing) water or wrap them in wet towels.
   - Apply damp cool cloths or ice wrapped in cloth to the neck, armpits, and groin where major blood vessels pass close to the surface.
   - Fan the patient vigorously by hand or with an electric fan to promote evaporative cooling.
4. **Hydration Caution:** Only give sips of cool water if the person is fully alert and conscious. Never force liquids down the throat of someone who is confused, vomiting, or semi-conscious.
