## Managing Acute Water Loss

In crisis encampments and disaster zones, diarrhoeal illnesses spread quickly due to crowded sanitation and compromised food hygiene. The principal danger of diarrhoea is acute dehydration: the dangerous loss of vital water and electrolytes from the human body.

---

## Recognising Dehydration Early

Be alert for these early and moderate signs of body fluid depletion:
- **In Children & Infants:** Dry mouth and tongue, crying without tears, sunken eyes, no wet nappies for over 3 hours, and unusual lethargy or irritability.
- **In Adults:** Extreme thirst, lightheadedness or dizziness upon standing, dark-coloured urine, and sudden weakness.

---

## Oral Rehydration Salts (ORS)

ORS is the standard, lifesaving medical intervention for restoring balanced body fluids.

### Preparing Government/Relief ORS Packets
1. Wash hands thoroughly with clean water and soap before preparation.
2. Pour the complete contents of one packet into **1 litre** of clean, boiled-and-cooled drinking water.
3. Mix thoroughly until all minerals and sugars have completely dissolved.
4. Give small, frequent sips to the affected individual throughout the day.
5. Discard any prepared solution that remains unused after **24 hours**, as bacteria can multiply in room-temperature electrolyte mixtures.

---

## Emergency Home-Based Sugar-Salt Solution

If standard manufactured ORS packets are not obtainable in your settlement:
- Take **1 litre of boiled and cooled drinking water**.
- Add **6 level teaspoons of sugar**.
- Add **half a level teaspoon of salt**.
- Stir thoroughly until fully dissolved. The fluid should taste no saltier than tears.
- Administer continually alongside clean breastmilk or light fluids.

---

## When to Seek Urgent Clinical Care

Seek emergency healthcare immediately if:
- The person passes bloody or black stools.
- Frequent vomiting prevents holding any fluid down.
- High fever accompanies the illness.
- The person becomes unresponsive, floppy, or difficult to awaken.
