## Teaching Preparedness Without Inducing Fear

Children learn best when emergency procedures are presented as confident games, skill badges, and cooperative challenges rather than frightening warnings. This 30-minute interactive lesson plan can be taught by parents, elder siblings, or community educators.

---

## Part 1: The Three Magic Words (Drop, Cover, Hold On)

- **The Game:** Play the "Statue and Turtle" drill.
- **The Concept:** When the instructor calls "Tremor!", children must immediately drop low like a turtle pulling into its shell.
- **Hands-On Activity:** Have each child crawl beneath a sturdy desk or table, hold tightly to one of the table legs with their hand, and tuck their head down to shield their eyes. Count to thirty together in loud, unison voices.

---

## Part 2: Emergency Contact Memory Challenge

Every child should know how to connect with family members if separated during evacuation:

1. **The Family Phone Song:** Turn a parent or guardian's 10-digit phone number into a rhythmic rhyme or simple song. Practice singing it together daily until every child can recite it from memory.
2. **Home Village and Landmark:** Teach children to state their full legal name, their village/ward name, and a major prominent landmark near their dwelling (e.g. "near the water tank", "beside the primary health clinic").
3. **Emergency Helpline Numbers:** Teach the national helpline number **112** as the one number for help across India.

---

## Part 3: The Safe Meeting Place

- Walk with children around the neighbourhood or shelter compound to select two distinct rendezvous points:
  - **Spot A (Near Home):** A prominent tree, lamppost, or community ground just outside the home for sudden evacuations like kitchen fires.
  - **Spot B (Neighbourhood Safe Zone):** A sturdy public building, sports ground, or school courtyard if the entire neighbourhood is evacuated.
- Emphasize to children: "If we ever get separated, do not wander or follow strangers into unknown areas. Go to our safe spot and wait for help."

---

*Note: Always follow instructions and advisories issued by your local disaster management authorities and emergency response teams.*
