## The Importance of Routine in Crises

Disasters disrupt normal life, separate families from familiar surroundings, and abruptly shut down formal schooling. For young children, regular daily routines provide psychological grounding, emotional safety, and a defense against trauma.

---

## 1. Establishing Predictable Daily Circles

- **Set a Consistent Morning Hour:** Even in a relief camp or temporary shelter, designate a specific 60-to-90-minute block each morning for quiet learning and storytelling.
- **Form Small Neighbourhood Cohorts:** Group four to six children of similar age under the guidance of an older sibling, adolescent volunteer, or retired teacher.
- **Low-Resource Materials:** You do not need expensive textbooks. Use slate chalkboards, scrap paper, cardboard cartons, smooth pebbles for counting, and sticks in clean sand for handwriting practice.

---

## 2. Interactive and Oral Learning Activities

Oral traditions and peer-led activities thrive even when all written school materials have been lost:

- **Mathematical Games:** Practice mental arithmetic, multiplication tables, estimation games (e.g. estimating the number of steps between tents), and time measurement.
- **Community Storytelling:** Encourage grandparents and elders in the camp to narrate cultural fables, historical stories, and folklore. Have children re-tell the narrative in their own words to strengthen language comprehension.
- **Nature and Environmental Observations:** Explore local geography, cloud formations, and local plant species. Science learning can be drawn directly from immediate surroundings.

---

## 3. Supporting Emotional Well-Being

Children absorb distress and anxiety from adults around them:
- **Encourage Creative Expression:** Provide paper and pencils or clay so children can draw or model what they feel. Drawing helps young minds process frightening memories.
- **Listen Reassuringly:** Answer questions about the emergency honestly, without exaggerating danger or making false promises.
- **Physical Play:** Ensure children have safe, designated areas for skipping, group singing, and cooperative games each afternoon. Physical activity reduces stress hormones and fosters communal resilience.

---

*Note: Always follow instructions and advisories issued by your local disaster management authorities and emergency response teams.*
