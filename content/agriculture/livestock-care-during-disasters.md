## Protecting Animals During Extreme Weather

Farm animals, cattle, goats, and poultry represent irreplaceable working assets and food security for rural families. During floods, cyclones, and landslides, domestic animals are highly vulnerable if kept confined.

---

## 1. Golden Rule: Never Leave Animals Tied Up

- When a flash flood or structural storm warning is issued, **untie livestock from stationary stakes, sheds, or ground tethers**.
- Tethered animals will drown or be crushed by collapsing thatch roofs if water surges rapidly. When untied, cattle and goats have strong innate swimming abilities and can instinctively locate elevated earth mounds.
- Move herds together toward communal elevated shelters or highway embankments well before roads become impassable.

---

## 2. Safe Fodder and Nutrition Management

- **Feed Contamination:** Never feed wet, fermented, or visibly silt-coated grass to cattle. Submerged green grass harbors fungal mycotoxins that cause deadly colic and bloat in ruminants.
- **Dry Fodder Reserves:** Store dry straw, hay bales, and bagged oil cakes high off the ground in wrapped plastic sheets. A few days of dry feed preserve rumen function until fresh grazing land dries.
- **Mineral Salts and Clean Water:** Livestock require clean, uncontaminated drinking water. Add a pinch of iodized salt or limestone powder to water troughs to replenish essential electrolytes.

---

## 3. Disease Prevention and Veterinary Vigilance

Following floodwaters, humid soil creates breeding environments for catastrophic bacterial and viral livestock epidemics:

- **Foot Rot and Thrush:** Keep animal hooves as dry as possible. If hooves show ulceration, clean them with a dilute potassium permanganate solution or clean salty water.
- **Vaccination Records:** Keep animal vaccination cards safe inside waterproof pouches alongside your family documents.
- **Carcass Disposal:** Deceased animals must be deeply buried (at least 1.5 to 2 metres deep) away from groundwater wells and covered with quicklime to prevent the transmission of anthrax, blackleg, or hemorrhagic septicemia.

---

*Note: Always follow instructions and advisories issued by your local disaster management authorities and emergency response teams.*
