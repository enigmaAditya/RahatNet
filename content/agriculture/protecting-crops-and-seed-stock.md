## Safeguarding Your Agricultural Future

A flood not only washes away current seasonal yields, but by ruining stored seed grain, it threatens a farming community's ability to replant in subsequent seasons. Taking defensive measures prior to heavy monsoons or flood surges protects family sustenance and capital.

---

## 1. Protecting Standing Crops

- **Early Drainage Trenching:** Dig shallow surface drainage channels along the perimeter and between row borders of fields to facilitate natural gravity drainage into nearby ditches or retention ponds.
- **Advance Harvesting:** If mature or nearly mature grain, pulses, or vegetables are standing in low-lying tracts, harvest them early even if moisture content is slightly higher than ideal. Premature grain can be artificially dried or used as fodder, whereas submerged grain will rot and germinate in the ear.
- **Earthing Up:** For tuber and root crops (such as potatoes, ginger, and turmeric), mound loose soil up around the root base to prevent water from collecting directly around sensitive crowns.

---

## 2. Preserving Certified Seed Stock

Preserving heirloom and certified seed varieties is essential for post-disaster livelihood restoration:

- **Airtight Hermetic Packaging:** Store dry seed grains in moisture-impermeable, airtight drums, metallic bins, or heavy polythene sacks double-sealed with thick cord or wax.
- **Elevated Platforms (Machans):** Never store grain bags directly on ground floors or earthen surfaces. Construct raised timber platforms or utilize ceiling rafters at least 2 metres above projected peak flood levels.
- **Moisture Desiccants:** Add dry neem leaves or clean wood ash inside storage drums before sealing to suppress fungal spore development and repel weevil infestations naturally.
- **Identification Tags:** Clearly label each sealed bag with crop variety, maturity period, and harvest date in permanent waterproof ink.

---

## Post-Harvest Grain Drying

If grain has been exposed to high air humidity or light rain, spread it immediately in thin layers on clean plastic tarpaulins in shaded, well-ventilated areas as soon as the weather breaks. Regular turning prevents localized heat build-up and sour mold fermentation.

---

*Note: Always follow instructions and advisories issued by your local disaster management authorities and emergency response teams.*
