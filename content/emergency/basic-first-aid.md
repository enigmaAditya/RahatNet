## First Aid Principles During Emergencies

Before delivering first aid, always check that the immediate surroundings are safe for both you and the casualty. Maintain calm, speak reassuringly, and send someone to seek professional medical personnel or call emergency helplines.

---

## Severe Bleeding Control

Uncontrolled bleeding is a leading cause of preventable trauma deaths. Acting promptly to stop blood loss is critical.

### Step 1: Direct Firm Pressure
- Place a clean cloth, sterile gauze, or clothing pad directly over the bleeding wound.
- Apply firm, continuous downward pressure using your palm or both hands.
- Maintain constant pressure for at least 5 to 10 minutes without lifting the dressing to look.

### Step 2: Elevation
- If there are no broken bones suspected in the injured limb, elevate the bleeding extremity above heart level while continuing firm pressure.

### Step 3: Secure the Dressing
- Secure the dressing firmly with a rolled bandage or cloth strip. Ensure the bandage is tight enough to hold pressure but not so tight that it stops pulse or turns extremities blue.
- If blood soaks through the dressing, do not remove the initial pad. Place additional absorbent cloths over it and apply firmer pressure.

## Thermal Burn Management

Burns damage skin barriers and make patients vulnerable to rapid fluid loss and severe infections.

### Step 1: Stop the Burning Process
- Extinguish active flames: remember **Stop, Drop, and Roll**.
- Remove the casualty from hot steam, boiling water, or heat sources.

### Step 2: Cool With Running Water
- Cool the burned area under clean, cool running water for **10 to 20 minutes**.
- Cooling relieves pain, prevents heat from burning deeper tissue, and limits swelling.
- **Never use ice or freezing water:** Extreme cold constricts blood vessels and worsens tissue necrosis.
- **Never apply home remedies:** Do not put ghee, butter, toothpaste, oil, or turmeric on raw burns; these trap heat and cause bacterial contamination.

### Step 3: Cover and Protect
- Cover the cooled burn loosely with clean, dry, lint-free cloth or sterile plastic wrap.
- Do not burst blisters. Blisters provide a natural protective barrier against bacterial infection.
