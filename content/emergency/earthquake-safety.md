## What to Do During Ground Shaking

When tremors begin, do not try to run outside while the ground is actively moving. Most injuries occur from falling debris, collapsing masonry, and shattering glass.

### If You Are Indoors: Drop, Cover, and Hold On
1. **Drop** down onto your hands and knees. This position protects you from being knocked over and allows you to stay low.
2. **Cover** your head and neck with your arms. If sturdy furniture like a heavy table or desk is nearby, crawl underneath it for shelter.
3. **Hold On** to your shelter with one hand and stay prepared to move with it until the shaking stops completely.
4. Stay away from glass windows, exterior walls, light fixtures, and heavy furniture that could topple over.

### If You Are in Bed
Stay where you are and protect your head with a pillow. Turning face down reduces your risk of injury from flying debris.

### If You Are Outdoors
Move immediately into an open area away from buildings, overhead electrical wires, street lights, and large trees. Once in an open space, crouch down and cover your head.

### If You Are in a Moving Vehicle
Safely pull over to the side of the road away from overpasses, bridges, overhead wires, and steep embankments. Stay inside the vehicle with the emergency brake engaged until ground movement ceases.

## After Shaking Stops: Immediate Actions

- **Check Yourself and Others:** Check for injuries before moving. Administer immediate first aid for minor cuts and bruises if trained.
- **Inspect for Hazards:** Smell for gas leaks. If you smell gas or suspect a ruptured pipe, turn off the main valve if accessible and leave the premises immediately. Do not flip electrical switches or light matches.
- **Beware of Aftershocks:** Secondary tremors can occur minutes, hours, or days after the main shock. Aftershocks can cause already weakened structures to collapse.
- **Exit Weakened Buildings:** Use stairways carefully. Never use elevators during or after a seismic event.
