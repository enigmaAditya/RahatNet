## Understanding Flood Hazards

Floods are among the most frequent natural disasters. Flash floods can develop rapidly without immediate visible rainfall in your immediate vicinity, particularly downstream of swollen rivers or broken embankments.

---

## When a Flood Warning Is Issued

- **Heed Early Warnings:** Move immediately to higher ground if advised by authorities or if you observe water levels rising rapidly.
- **Secure Essential Utilities:** Turn off the main electrical circuit breaker and LPG gas cylinder valves before leaving your dwelling.
- **Protect Critical Possessions:** Move vital documents, medications, dry rations, and basic electronics to upper floors or waterproof containers.
- **Unplug Electrical Appliances:** Do not touch electrical equipment if you are wet or standing in water.

---

## The Rule of Floodwaters: Turn Around, Don't Drown

1. **Never Walk Through Moving Water:** Just six inches (15 cm) of fast-flowing water can sweep an adult off their feet. If you must walk in standing water, use a sturdy stick or pole to probe the ground ahead for drop-offs or open drains.
2. **Never Drive Into Flooded Roads:** Most flood-related fatalities occur in submerged vehicles. A foot of flowing water can float many passenger cars, and two feet can carry away SUVs and pickup trucks.
3. **Avoid Submerged Bridges and Culverts:** Floodwaters frequently scour the foundational supports of rural bridges without visible surface damage.

---

## Precautions for Evacuation Shelters

- Keep a battery-operated torch and whistle handy to signal search and rescue teams.
- Wear sturdy boots or enclosed shoes to prevent lacerations from hidden glass, submerged debris, or encounters with displaced reptiles.
- Do not consume food or unsealed liquids that have come into direct contact with floodwaters.

---

*Note: Always follow instructions and advisories issued by your local disaster management authorities and emergency response teams.*
